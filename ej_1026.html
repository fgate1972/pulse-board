EURJPY, Monday 26 Oct — why the book sold at 23:00 where it had bought at 10:00

shadow · the book as set · prices from the LHFX feed · times Dallas
Short answer: nothing in the market changed between the 10:00 buy and the 23:00 sell — price was 176.17 and then 176.11, inside the same 176.0–176.4 box all day. What changed was the 1-hour trend reading. It flipped from up to down because Monday was so quiet that the 1-hour ATR shrank from 0.207 to 0.139, and the trend rule measures a turn in units of that ATR. A 0.30 dip that wasn't a turn at 10:00 became one at 23:00. With the daily trend down and the 4-hour trend up, the 1-hour reading is the tie-breaker, so the grade went from "A buy" to "A sell" on a flat market.

5 · What this teaches, and what I recommend

Two weaknesses, both real, both visible only because the book is running:
1. The 1-hour trend flips on quiet days. The turn threshold is 1.5 × the current 1-hour ATR, and on a dead day that shrinks, so ordinary chop inside a box reads as a new trend. On a symbol where the daily and 4-hour disagree, the 1-hour alone decides the grade, which means the grade on a quiet day is close to random.
2. The book can hold both directions on one symbol. When the grade flips like this, it ends up long and short the same pair, paying two spreads for a net position near zero.
Recommendation (tested before anything changes, per the frozen-week rule): run two fixes against the 29-week test and this week's shadow, separately and together. (a) Measure the 1-hour turn against the 1-hour ATR averaged over 20 days, not the last 14 bars, so a quiet day can't shrink the threshold. (b) Don't open a position against an open position on the same symbol. If either holds in both halves of the test, it goes in at the weekend re-choice; nothing changes mid-week.
